# ABBV — AbbVie Inc — Buffett Framework Analysis

## 1. Business Overview
AbbVie is a US biopharmaceutical company (NASDAQ) spun off from Abbott Laboratories in 2013. Revenue is derived from three pillars: immunology (~35%, led by Skyrizi and Rinvoq — fast-growing), legacy Humira (~45%, now facing accelerating biosimilar erosion), and oncology/aesthetics (~20%, including Botox via the 2020 Allergan acquisition at $63B). AbbVie is a top-10 global pharma company by revenue and holds a commanding position in the autoimmune disease drug market.

## 2. Current Metrics (approx. as of May 2026)
- P/E ratio: ~17–19x
- P/B ratio: Negative book value (leveraged buyout of Allergan eroded book equity)
- ROE: Very high optically (negative book base inflates ratio — not meaningful)
- Revenue growth (3yr CAGR): ~4–6% (Humira biosimilar headwind partially offset by Skyrizi/Rinvoq growth)
- Debt/Equity: ~5x (elevated; direct consequence of $63B Allergan acquisition financing)
- Dividend yield: ~3.5% ($6.00+/share; one of the most consistent dividend payers in pharma)

## 3. Moat Assessment
**Moat type identified:** Mixed — patent moat (temporary) on Humira/Skyrizi/Rinvoq; brand/switching-cost moat on Botox (aesthetics); no single dominant moat type
**Closest Berkshire analog:** No direct analog; closest in spirit to Johnson & Johnson (healthcare + brand diversification), which Buffett has held indirectly through index-like exposures
**Buffett quote applied:** "The single most important decision in evaluating a business is pricing power. If you've got the power to raise prices without losing business to a competitor, you've got a very good business." — Warren Buffett, 2011 FCIC interview
**Moat verdict:** MODERATE
**Reasoning:** Botox in aesthetics possesses a genuine brand/switching-cost moat — physicians and consumers have deep familiarity and trust with the brand that generics have not eroded meaningfully. However, the core immunology franchise relies on patent protection that is time-limited; Humira lost exclusivity and biosimilar penetration is accelerating. Skyrizi and Rinvoq extend the runway but themselves will face patent cliffs, creating a treadmill dynamic rather than a durable compounding moat. Buffett has historically avoided businesses requiring constant reinvention of their core earnings engine.

## 4. Management Assessment
**Capital allocation grade:** B
**Owner-mindset signals:**
- Rick Gonzalez executed the transformative Allergan acquisition (2020) to diversify away from Humira dependency — strategically sound even if expensive
- Unwavering dividend commitment through the Humira biosimilar headwind signals owner-oriented cash return discipline
- Pipeline investment in Skyrizi/Rinvoq has demonstrated early success (both drugs generating multi-billion revenues ahead of analyst expectations)
**Red flags:**
- D/E of ~5x is dangerously high; a downturn in Skyrizi/Rinvoq revenues or a pipeline failure could create a debt servicing crisis
- Allergan acquisition was priced at a premium that required optimistic synergy assumptions — classic "institutional imperative" deal risk
- Negative book equity makes balance sheet fragile
**Management verdict:** WATCH
**Reasoning:** Gonzalez has demonstrated strategic foresight (Allergan) and dividend discipline, earning partial credit as a capital allocator. However, Buffett's framework is explicit that "leverage amplifies both gains and losses, and it can turn a temporary setback into a catastrophe" — a D/E of 5x on a business facing a patent cliff is precisely this kind of fragility. The management team deserves credit for execution but the balance sheet risk is not consistent with Buffett's preference for fortress financial positions.

## 5. Price Assessment
**Current P/E vs Buffett benchmark (≤20x preferred, ≤15x ideal):** At 17–19x, ABBV sits just within Buffett's outer limit; it is not cheap but not egregiously expensive
**Intrinsic value comment:** Roughly fair for the moat quality — the Skyrizi/Rinvoq growth story justifies a slight premium over a pure Humira-decline scenario, but the D/E of 5x and patent-cliff risk argue against paying a full "wonderful company" multiple
**Price verdict:** WATCH
**Reasoning:** A P/E of 17–19x is acceptable only if Skyrizi and Rinvoq sustain current growth trajectories for the next decade — an assumption that requires meaningful confidence in a complex pharmaceutical pipeline. Buffett's purchase of Coca-Cola at ~15x in 1988 was justified by near-certain 10-year earnings visibility; AbbVie's earnings a decade out are materially less certain. At 15x or below, the risk/reward would be more compelling.

## 6. Circle of Competence Note
AbbVie sits at the edge of Buffett's documented circle — the Botox aesthetics franchise is consumer-brand-like and relatively predictable, but the immunology/oncology drug pipeline is not. Buffett has explicitly stated that pharmaceutical pipelines, clinical trial outcomes, and patent cliff timing are "outside my circle of competence" (paraphrase, multiple shareholder meetings), and ABBV's 10-year earnings depend heavily on pipeline success that cannot be predicted with high confidence.

AbbVie's Skyrizi/Rinvoq pipeline succession partially replaces Humira's patent cliff, giving it a rolling pharmaceutical moat analogous to what Buffett called "the ability to raise prices without losing customers" — Botox in aesthetics comes closest to that standard. However, pharma pipelines are binary-outcome bets, and Buffett's documented avoidance of pure pharma ("We don't invest in things where we can't predict the earnings in 10 years") puts this firmly at EDGE of his circle.
